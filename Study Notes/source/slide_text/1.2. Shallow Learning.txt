K-fold cross-validation

 Common practice to have a higher degree of invariance to
  the validation/test split.

    Separate the data into k folds
    Do k training-validation rounds. A usual value for k is 10.
    The validation/training sets are changed each round.
    Metrics are averaged over all folds.
    If k=N, the we have leave-one-out cross-validation or LOOCV

Precision and Recall (some intuition)

   The girlfriend/boyfriend example:

      Your girlfriend/boyfriend makes birthday presents for you every
        year.

      One year she/he asks: Do you remember all my presents from
        the last 10 years?

      Precision is how many are correct from the ones you said you
        remember.

      Recall is how many correct you can remember from all correct
        ones.

      How can we have 100% precision or recall? What happens to
        the other?

      What is the relation to the classification threshold?

   The airport security example:

      FP and FN: Have both errors the same consequences?
      How can we have 100% precision or recall?

   Do you need precision, recall or both?

      The solution is, most often, a compromise...

 Ensembling

 The machine learning equivalent to the wisdom of the crowd

    Aggregating predictions from several models improve over the individual ones

 Example (from Géron 2019):

    Aggregating the votes of 1,000 binary classifiers with individual accuracies of 51%
      gives us an accuracy of 75%!

 Practical trick: when, at the end of a machine learning project, you
  trained several good models, combine their predictions to obtain
  an even better one.

Ensemble II - Boosting

 AdaBoost:

    Train weak model 1 (tree, linear, etc.)
    Increase weight of points wrongly

      classified by model 1
    Train weak model 2 on

      weighted data
    Repeat process and build

      a cascade of models.
    Take decisions based on

      ensemble of models.

 Gradient boosting:

    Generalized to any loss (regression,
      classification...)

    Fit residuals of previous model,
      use learning rate to combine models-

    Popular example: XGBoost
