Training RNNs: backpropagation through time
● Unroll the RNN over T steps → a deep feed-forward network whose layers
share Wxh, Whh, Why
● Total loss = sum of the per-step losses:
● Standard backprop (4.3) on the unrolled graph. Gradients of a shared weight
add up over time:
○ ∂⁺: immediate derivative, treating hk−1 as a constant
● Truncated BPTT: backpropagate through the last K steps only (memory,
speed)
Goodfellow, Bengio, Courville. Deep Learning, ch. 10.2 · R. Pascanu, T. Mikolov, Y. Bengio. On the difficulty of training recurrent neural networks. ICML 2013

=== PDF PAGE 15 ===
Why gradients vanish or explode
● Each factor of the product is a Jacobian:
● Since |tanh′| ≤ 1:
● σmax < 1 → contributions from far-away steps
shrink exponentially: vanishing gradient, long-
range dependencies are not learned
● σmax > 1 → they can grow exponentially:
exploding gradient (loss spikes, NaN)
R. Pascanu, T. Mikolov, Y. Bengio. On the difficulty of training recurrent neural networks. ICML 2013 (σ_max = largest singular value)

=== PDF PAGE 16 ===
Fixing the gradient problems
● Exploding → gradient clipping: rescale the gradient if its norm is too
large
○ Keeps the direction, limits the step. One line in PyTorch:
torch.nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_
● Vanishing → change the architecture
○ Gated units (LSTM, GRU): an additive path for information and gradients, like the
skip connections of ResNets
○ Helpers: orthogonal initialisation of W hh, layer normalisation
○ Truncated BPTT saves memory and time but does not fix vanishing: it cuts the
long-range gradients
● Or remove the long chain of multiplications altogether
○ Temporal convolutions (TCN) and attention: short paths between any two time
steps
R. Pascanu, T. Mikolov, Y. Bengio. On the difficulty of training recurrent neural networks. ICML 2013

Why gates help, and the GRU
● The LSTM cell state is updated additively:
○ Forget gate ft ≈ 1 → information and gradients cross many steps without being
multiplied by Whh
● GRU (Cho et al., 2014): lighter variant, no separate cell state; update
gate zt, reset gate rt
○ 3 weight blocks instead of 4 → fewer parameters; similar accuracy on many tasks
(nn.LSTM, nn.GRU)
K. Cho et al. Learning phrase representations using RNN encoder –decoder for statistical machine translation. EMNLP 2014 · biases omitted · conventions differ:
PyTorch nn.GRU swaps z and 1 − z

=== PDF PAGE 29 ===
Training the decoder: teacher forcing
● Training: feed the ground-truth previous token y*t−1 as decoder input, not the
model's own guess
○ Every step gets the correct context → stable training, no sampling needed; steps run in
parallel only in non-recurrent decoders (5.2)
● Loss = cross-entropy of the next token (maximum likelihood, as in 1.2):
● Inference: no ground truth → feed back the model's own predictions (greedy
or beam search)
● Train/test mismatch = exposure bias: an early mistake puts the decoder in
states it never saw
○ Remedy: scheduled sampling (gradually feed own predictions during training)
● Same recipe as next-token training of GPT-style models (5.2)
R. J. Williams, D. Zipser. A learning algorithm for continually running fully recurrent neural networks. Neural Computation, 1989 · S. Bengio et al. Scheduled sampling.
NeurIPS 2015
